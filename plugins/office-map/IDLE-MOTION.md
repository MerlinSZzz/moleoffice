# Idle movement

A participating agent needs loaded, coherent people and work facts with a known shared revision. Pending, failed, stale, missing or working facts do not establish idleness. No additional heavy read is created for movement.

At most two agents participate in one idle encounter, and at most one body walks at a time. The other participant reserves its current position. Eligible agents and clear destinations rotate after a completed outing; a lone agent can walk out and back without a partner. The route worker checks the moving look's union of standing, walking and gesture frame bounds in both facings against occupied bodies and visible artwork throughout every segment. Unknown bounds refuse the route. Wall alpha metadata is tied to the corresponding catalog pixel hash; an unknown or mismatched wall uses its full visible rectangle. Page-side artwork bounds are built cooperatively and discarded on scene, pack or lifecycle changes.

Recoloured walking and play frames use the pose-specific clothing masks and the saved recipe. Their preparation shares the existing decode owner, frame cache and source cap; a failed preparation prevents movement. Quiet mode, reduced motion, a hidden or inactive page, a changed scene and disposal cancel animation and route work. An away participant's home remains reserved until the outing ends, including during cancellation. The decorative paper is drawn only when the whole visible arc clears the supplied furniture, walls and bodies; the gestures remain when it is blocked. It is not evidence of communication.

Walking repeats A, pass, B, pass at 200 ms per frame. Paired play alternates wave and cheer every 1,200 ms, with the partners in opposite phases. The paper moves out and back over 2,400 ms on an arc, when its full envelope is clear. The synthetic 17-frame paper studies in the round's output show the earlier 96-unit candidate and are not captures of the current 160-unit placement. Integrated map footage and visual acceptance remain separate from those studies.

Who is asleep comes from the office's asleep read. A picture tool may replace that read with made-up sleepers, and the page then labels them as made up beside the map and on each sleeper's nameplate. When the read fails, nobody is drawn asleep and the page says so. A sleeper uses the matching look when its sprite is available, and recoloured sleep frames use their own masks and anchors. Only requested sleep sources are loaded, sharing the existing source budget with motion; when those frames cannot be prepared, the standing fallback remains available.
