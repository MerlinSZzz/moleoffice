# Round chapter delivery, item 19(a)

Old wording: A round passes its task brief verbatim. A check obtains its chapters from the agent's current registration or role card and today's office chapter records.

New wording: A round derives its kind from the brief or an available delegation, without requiring a hand-written sidecar; it may declare a `territory` string[] path list in `<brief>.round.json`. Reviewable JSON policy selects exact CODE chapters and local sandbox brief sections. The round prints the source and section list, saves version 1 reading evidence in `run.data.extra.roundChapters`, and delivers the same saved text through every runtime. The original task brief remains byte-for-byte present. The check and pre-seal reminder use this saved selection; file, policy, card and record drift cannot add rules. An absent or unknown kind delivers CODE.md and LINE-common.md in full with an explicit notice. For an absent or unknown kind, and for a kind whose policy gives TERRITORIES.md as the full file, TERRITORIES.md is narrowed only when the sandbox line and fallback rule are unambiguous, and a missing line or fallback leaves all three files full. A policy's explicit TERRITORIES.md selection is kept as the policy names it when no heading names the sandbox, because no round-start pack then replaces it; when a heading names the sandbox, it is kept only if it equals that line's section and fallback rule, and otherwise all three files are full. A negated code instruction, such as one to change nothing in a named file, does not declare a kind. Conflicting sandbox headings refuse the start. Bad policy, missing files, ambiguous or absent selected headings fail visibly. Old runs without a manifest retain their existing behavior.

Why: The delivered rules and the rules used to judge a round must be the same reviewable evidence. Mapping belongs in data, and narrowing must never happen by accident.

Behavioral proof: Execute the real commandRound with synthetic filing/launch boundaries for Codex, OpenCode, both Claude argument paths and dsh. Assert saved evidence exists before launch and the runtime receives the derived brief. Select made-up Markdown by injected kind and territory policy, checking exact list and text, descendants, fenced examples, local file resolution, explicit full-file fallback, and named errors. Read that evidence through the real inspection readers and prompt after changing policy/files/card defaults. Reject a verdict citing an undelivered chapter while retaining its actual reported read levels. No live database or real runtime is used. Prove control, guarded mutation red and restore with named tests.

Upgrade acknowledgement: Before a narrowed round starts, the existing check reading must acknowledge the saved manifest version, a canonical hash of the complete snapshot and the exact chapter list/levels in its existing chapters.says field. A legacy reply may continue only after all three files were delivered in full, with an explicit notice. Null removal by partial writes and object property order do not change the acknowledgement. A wrong hash/list fails visibly.

A 200 response with missing or malformed chapter reading is unreadable, not evidence of a legacy office. Full-file compatibility requires a structurally readable old chapters object, including its named list, mustRead ladder entries, from and says fields. Unreadable replies fail visibly.

Runtime-neighbour fixtures provide confined rule sources and a readable chapter reply. Their round checks retain the exact task inside the derived input, require all fixture rules and the saved full-file manifest, and keep model, effort, identity and permission assertions. The MCP list includes the declared picture tools as well as the office request tool.

## A round reads only its own items, item 19(e)

Old wording: A round reads its line's items in `briefs/REQUIREMENTS.md`, the whole file whatever the round works on.

New wording: A declaration may name a `line` and the `items` the round works on. The round start asks the office for that line's backlog before anything is filed or started. A declaration that names items gets them whole, in the line's order, and every other item of the line as one line with its number, state and record id. A declaration that names no items gets every item that is not done or dropped whole. An office with no backlog door, or with no items for the line, is said in one sentence that points to the file, and the round starts. If the sandbox has no such file, the start stops by name, because that sentence would point the round to a file that is not there. Any other answer stops the start by name. A declaration with no line is delivered byte for byte as before. The reading is saved in the manifest, and the brief made from the saved manifest is the brief delivered.

Why: A round of one item was sent to the whole requirement file, about 24 KB. Its own item whole and one line for each other item is about 5 KB. In a replay of two real rounds of one item against a made-up office, the round's start gives 4,826 and 4,607 bytes; over the whole round, the two rounds read 23,904 and 5,969 bytes of the file. A declaration that names no item gives every item whole until the items' states are recorded, which is slightly more than the file. The office's backlog items are the record of the line's work; the file is read only while the office has none.

Behavioral proof: a scratch office in process, with made-up backlog items filed through the records door, read through `readTheRoundsBacklog` and saved through `saveRoundChapters` to the check door. The older office is a stand-in on a port of its own that answers 404 with the server's own answer for a door it does not have, asked through the launcher's own ask while nothing listens yet. If the server's words for that answer change, the round's start no longer matches them and these tests are red. The filing tool runs against a scratch office through a fetch stand-in, never the live office.

## A start carries the standing stages of its flows, item 22

Old wording: A round's brief and a worker's brief carry the standing rules of the line only when the person who wrote the brief copied them in. A worker reads its brief, not the line's rules, so a rule left out of a worker brief is not given to that worker.

New wording: A flow's stage may be marked `standing`, true or false; left out, it is not marked. When the launcher starts a round, a worker or an `exec` run of an agent with a brief, it reads the agent's role from the office, then every flow the office lists for that role, and gives the start the words of each stage marked standing, with the flow's title, id and the revision it read. A worker gets them before its brief, so its brief still ends with its own last step. A round gets them right after its brief, and its saved chapter selection names each flow and the revision it read, so a check can tell which words the round was given; a saved selection whose standing text is not the text its flows give is refused when it is read. A stage changed later is given in its new words at the next start. A command whose last word is the brief's text, for Codex or OpenCode, gets the same text in that word; such a command whose last word is not the brief's text is refused. When no stage is marked, the brief is delivered byte for byte as before, and the start says so in one line. When the office refuses the reads or does not answer, or answers in a shape it cannot read, the start goes on without them and says why in one sentence. A stage marked standing that cannot be read stops the start.

Why: On 30 October 2026 a worker brief left out one of the line's standing rules: print the office variables only with the tool that masks them. The person's words that morning were to record such rules in the office's flow, and let the office's programs keep them, not a person's memory. A stopped start costs a round, and a missing standing rule costs one part of a brief, so only a marked stage that cannot be read stops a start.

Behavioral proof: a scratch office in process, with made-up flows filed through the records door, read through the real `commandWorker`, `commandExec` and `commandRound` of the launcher with made-up filing and start boundaries. Every read goes through the launcher's own ask that waits while the office restarts. The worker's brief names no tool for the office variables, and the stage that names it is delivered. The round's saved selection is acknowledged by the office's own check door. An office of today's shape, with no mark, is a stand-in on a port of its own. Refused, missing and unreadable answers, and marked stages that cannot be read, are made-up answers of that ask. No live office or real runtime is used.

## A round's start gives the chapters at outline

Old wording: The round was given the saved selection's text, CODE.md whole.

New wording: The round is given CODE.md at outline (each chapter's title and first line, the door and the path named), and every other part as before; the saved selection and the check are unchanged, so a check still judges against the saved selection and a round reads the full chapter it needs with `GET /api/office-regulations`.

Why: A supervisor's start was over 70 KB, most of it CODE.md; the target is brief, card and outline under 35 KB.

Behavioral proof: a made-up sandbox under the system temp folder, holding a made-up 12 KB brief with no declaration, a made-up 5.7 KB LINE-common.md, a heading of its own TERRITORIES.md naming it, a made-up NEXT.md, and a worktree that holds this checkout's real CODE.md. The real `prepareRoundChapters` builds the start. The delivered brief, and the brief rebuilt from the saved record, are equal and under 35 000 bytes; it names `GET /api/office-regulations`; it holds each chapter heading of the saved code source once, read from the saved sections rather than a typed list; the saved selection still holds the code source's sections whole and `savedChaptersLine` and `roundChaptersText` answer as before; and a policy selecting two chapters gives those two at outline and no other chapter heading. No live office or real runtime is used.
